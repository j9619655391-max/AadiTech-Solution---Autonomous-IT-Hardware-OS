## Purpose and trust boundary

Phase 1BF can recognize locally consistent Ed25519-signed **candidate** revocation publications and persist append-only claimant history while maintaining a Phase 1BE emergency suspect-witness freeze. The risk is that an attacker presents fresh-looking **CRL/OCSP** hash labels, a self-generated public key, an Object Lock "DENIED" hash and encrypted backup restoration timings, then attempts to turn internal validation into authorized external trust.

Phase 1BG introduces three separately claimed auditor approval lanes, a strict cross-layer digest-only receipt, source-freshness checks and a locally durable replay/rollback/fork/issuer-key quarantine. It **does not** fetch, parse or independently verify real X.509 CRLs or OCSP responses, authenticate trust roots, retrieve cloud provider attestations, perform backups or prove external independence. The current `REVOKED_CLAIMED` flag and synthetic signatures are never asserted as genuine live PKIX revocation.

2. **Candidate auditor separation:** distinct `REVOCATION_AUDITOR`, `STORAGE_AUDITOR`, `DR_AUDITOR` Ed25519 signed attestations must cover the **same exact domain-separated bytes**. Attestations have unique claimed administrator, source, and SPKI. They cannot reuse any claimed Phase 1BD witness, provider or SECURITY/OPERATIONS/OWNER key/control, nor the Phase 1BF publisher. A canonical sorted pin-set fingerprint prevents signature ordering from changing custody identity. 3. **Freshness and binding:** signed manifest must be current within its narrow claimed UTC validity window; claimed CRL and OCSP nextUpdate must be after thisUpdate/producedAt, must not expire before the audit validity, and candidate CRL digest cannot equal the previous revocation snapshot.
